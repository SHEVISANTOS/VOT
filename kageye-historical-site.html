About this excursion

Located 40 km northeast of Mwanza in the village of Kayenze, Kageye is a tranquil fishing community that was once the most important port on the southern shore of Lake Victoria in the late 19th century. The site was once a centre for the slave trade until missionaries ended it in 1878, and is today a tree-filled meadow of monuments and graves managed by the Sukuma Museum — a quiet, reflective half-day trip that traces the area's Sukuma, Arab and missionary history.

  • Traces of one of the oldest Sukuma chiefdoms
  • Remnants of former Arab settlements
  • Slave market remnants — a centre for the slave trade until missionaries ended it in 1878
  • Graves of missionaries and travelers
  • Remnants from different historical periods
  • Once the most important lake port on Lake Victoria's southern shore in the late 19th century

Good to Know

Requirements & what's included

Do I need to book this in advance? +

We recommend telling us a few days ahead so we can arrange the 40 km transfer and a guide, but the trip can often be added to your schedule at short notice if you're already volunteering with us — just ask the VOT team.

What should I bring or wear? +

Comfortable walking shoes, sun protection and a water bottle for the meadow site. As Kageye includes graves and historical monuments, we ask visitors to be quiet and respectful while exploring.

Can I visit if I'm in Mwanza on a short trip, not a full placement? +

Yes — this excursion is open to current volunteers and short-term visitors alike. Contact us and we'll arrange the visit around your dates.
